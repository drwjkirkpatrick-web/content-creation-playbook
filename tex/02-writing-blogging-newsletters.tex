\section*{Opening and Structure}
\begin{itemize}[label=\tlabel]
\tip{1. Write the headline or subject line first}{Specific and curiosity-honest beats clever or clickbaity; the promise sets the article's contract.}
\tip{2. Hook the preview text}{The first sentence of an email shows in the inbox - write it as if the delete button is next to it.}
\tip{3. Keep paragraphs to 2-3 sentences}{Subheadings, bullets, bold key phrases, and white space respect the reader's time.}
\tip{4. Stay under 300-500 words for email}{Newsletters link out to depth; they don't reproduce it.}
\end{itemize}
\section*{Substance and Voice}
\begin{itemize}[label=\tlabel]
\tip{5. Use concrete details over generic claims}{Specific numbers, names, and moments are what readers remember and repeat.}
\tip{6. Include failures with the wins}{Vulnerability builds trust; connect individual stories to universal experiences.}
\tip{7. Bold every unclear term and link it}{If a reader might not know a concept, hyperlink the explanation inline.}
\tip{8. Curate ruthlessly}{Deep insight on two topics beats surface coverage of ten.}
\end{itemize}
\section*{Conversion and Cadence}
\begin{itemize}[label=\tlabel]
\tip{9. One primary CTA per piece}{Make secondary actions hypertext, not buttons; the ask must fit the content's natural conclusion.}
\tip{10. Segment your list}{By interest, engagement level, or journey stage - then tailor subject lines and intros to each group.}
\tip{11. A/B test subject lines and placements}{Test one element at a time; you were sending something either way.}
\tip{12. Draft ahead on a fixed schedule}{Never send just to hit the calendar; send only when you have something worth the reader's attention.}
\end{itemize}
