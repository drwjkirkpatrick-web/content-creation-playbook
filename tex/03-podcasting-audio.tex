\section*{Recording}
\begin{itemize}[label=\tlabel]
\tip{1. Record in a soft, quiet room}{Rugs, curtains, and closets full of clothes absorb echoes; hard reflective rooms are the top audio killer.}
\tip{2. Mic 4-6 inches away, slightly off-axis}{Angle the mic 15-30 degrees off-center and add a pop filter to tame plosives.}
\tip{3. Always wear headphones}{They prevent remote-guest echo and let you catch plosives and breath noise live.}
\tip{4. Record separate tracks per speaker}{The decision pays for itself the first time you edit a crossover conversation.}
\tip{5. Mute yourself when others speak}{'Mm-hmm' interruptions are annoying to hear and miserable to edit out.}
\tip{6. Record a test clip first}{Thirty seconds of playback beats an hour of unfixable noise.}
\end{itemize}
\section*{Structure}
\begin{itemize}[label=\tlabel]
\tip{7. Cold-open with the best moment}{Preview the strongest 15-30 seconds before the intro.}
\tip{8. Keep the intro under 60 seconds}{Branding, host name, and topic framing - then get out of the way.}
\tip{9. Re-order for narrative, not chronology}{Move the strongest answer earlier; the conversation doesn't have to play in recorded order.}
\tip{10. Ask open-ended, researched questions}{Study the guest's work; ask for stories, not yes/no answers.}
\end{itemize}
\section*{Editing}
\begin{itemize}[label=\tlabel]
\tip{11. Content edit before technical processing}{Tighten structure first - EQ on audio you're about to cut is wasted work.}
\tip{12. Trim the runway before good answers}{Cut dead starts and framing that buries the question; target -16 LUFS for stereo.}
\end{itemize}
