\section*{Composition and Light}
\begin{itemize}[label=\tlabel]
\tip{1. One dominant subject per image}{Everything else supports the focal point; if everything fights equally, nothing wins.}
\tip{2. Place subjects on the rule-of-thirds grid}{Faces near an intersection, not dead center; fill 30-60\% of the frame.}
\tip{3. Shoot flat, even light on faces}{Clarity beats drama at small sizes; add shadow in post, fix underexposure never.}
\tip{4. Separate subject from background}{Tonal contrast, a subtle blur, or a subject stroke; keep backgrounds quieter than the subject.}
\tip{5. Use diagonal energy}{Tilted heads and corner-to-corner lines read as movement; static horizontals read as passive.}
\tip{6. Leave breathing room for platform crops}{Vertical-first for social; keep key elements away from edges.}
\end{itemize}
\section*{Design Details}
\begin{itemize}[label=\tlabel]
\tip{7. Limit text to 3-4 bold words}{Heavy-weight font, outlined or shadowed, in the upper third; text is a supporting actor.}
\tip{8. Stick to 2-3 colors}{Complementary pairs (blue/orange, yellow/purple) guide focus; consistency builds brand.}
\tip{9. Push expressions past comfort}{What looks over-the-top live reads natural at thumbnail size; clear eyes win clicks.}
\tip{10. Apply the 3-element rule}{Face, key object, short text - nothing else in the frame.}
\end{itemize}
\section*{Testing}
\begin{itemize}[label=\tlabel]
\tip{11. Preview at 120 pixels before publishing}{If the subject doesn't read on a phone screen, crop tighter or simplify.}
\tip{12. Change one design variable per test}{Composition, lighting, or expression - isolate the cause of the click-rate change.}
\end{itemize}
